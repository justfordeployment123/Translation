Fourth: Fulfilling the various rights of the Muslims, for example: visiting the sick, following the funeral, being gentle with them, praying for them and asking forgiveness for them, being honest in dealings, not cheating them, and not consuming their wealth wrongfully.

[en]
Fifth: Not spying on the Muslims and on Muslim governments, not passing their secrets to the enemy, relieving their hardship, and making peace between them.

[en]
Sixth: Holding fast to the Jama'ah of the Muslims and to their authority and government, not forming factions apart from them, and cooperating with them in righteousness and piety, and in enjoining good and forbidding evil.

[en]
Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah hold that hatred and enmity for the sake of Allah also require certain things, which are as follows:

[en]
First: Hating shirk (associating partners with Allah) and the polytheists, disbelief and the disbelievers, and holding enmity towards them in the heart.

[en]
Second: Not taking the disbelievers as friends at heart, and keeping completely apart from them, even if they are one's own relatives.

[en]
Third: Emigrating from the lands of disbelief, and not travelling to the lands of disbelief without need. If one is compelled to go, one must make sure that nothing there stands in the way of practising the distinctive rites of the religion.

[en]
Fourth: Completely avoiding and keeping away from resembling the disbelievers in the religious and worldly things that are particular to them. The religious things are such as their religious rites (their distinctive signs and practices), and the worldly characteristics are those forbidden in our Shariah, such as their forbidden ways of eating, drinking and dressing, and their other customs and things that are not common among Muslims.
