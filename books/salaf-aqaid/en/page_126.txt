[hen]
The Second Principle: The Reality of Faith

[en]
The definition of faith according to Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah: According to Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, faith is affirmation by the heart, declaration by the tongue and action by the working limbs of the body. Faith increases through obedience to Allah the Exalted and decreases through disobedience to Him.

[en]
Faith is both speech and action: speech of the heart and of the tongue, and action of the heart, the tongue and the working limbs of the body.

[fen]
Linguistically, faith means affirmation, showing submission and acknowledgement. In the Shariah, faith means all the outward and inward acts of obedience. The inward acts of obedience mean the affirmation of the heart, while the obligatory and recommended acts of the Shariah that the human body performs are the outward carrying out of the divine commands. In short, faith is that which settles firmly in the heart and is confirmed by a person's deeds. As a result of faith, a person carries out the divine commands and refrains from what Allah has forbidden. If knowledge is not accompanied by action, such knowledge is of no benefit. If knowledge alone could benefit anyone, it would certainly have benefited Iblis, for he knew that Allah is One and has no partner. The implications of this knowledge, too, were beyond doubt in his view, but when he was commanded by Allah the Exalted to prostrate to Adam, peace be upon him, he refused, was arrogant and was counted among the disbelievers. The knowledge he had of the Oneness of Allah did not help him, because knowledge devoid of action has no worth with Allah the Exalted. This was the understanding of the Righteous Predecessors in this matter. In the Noble Quran, too, faith is not mentioned without action being mentioned.
