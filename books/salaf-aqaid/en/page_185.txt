Every Muslim must, on the basis of evidence, leave one school and adopt another. A student of knowledge who is able to compare the evidence of the imams must, in a given issue, leave the position of one imam and adopt the position of another on the basis of evidence, if he considers that the other's evidence is stronger, even if in some other issue the evidence of the other imam is preferable.

[fen]
A second definition: taqlid is accepting someone's word without knowing its evidence. Another definition: turning to a word whose speaker has no evidence for it. A follower (muqallid) is one who follows a person in all his sayings and actions without knowing his evidence, and does not go outside the circle of that person's sayings, and who moreover believes that no one else's word can possibly be right, even if the other person's word is proven to him.

[fen]
There is no disagreement among the people of knowledge that taqlid is not knowledge, nor can a follower (muqallid) be called a scholar. Allah the Exalted condemned taqlid and forbade it in many verses of the Quran. He said: And when it is said to them, “Come to what Allah has revealed and to the Messenger,” they say, “Sufficient for us is that upon which we found our fathers.” Even though their fathers knew nothing, nor were they guided? (al-Ma'idah 5:104) The scholars of the Salaf and the mujtahid imams all forbade taqlid, because taqlid is one of the causes of the Ummah's disagreements and of its backwardness. All good, unity and harmony lie in following (ittiba') and in turning to the Quran and the Sunnah at times of disagreement. This is why we see that the noble Companions did not follow any one particular Companion by taqlid in all matters. This was also the way of the four imams (Imam Abu Hanifah, Imam Malik, Imam ash-Shafi'i and Imam Ahmad, may Allah have mercy on them). They were not partisan for their own opinions. When a hadith of the Messenger, peace be upon him, came before them, they would give up their own opinion. They also forbade others from blind taqlid without knowing the evidence. Thus Imam Abu Hanifah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: “If a hadith is proven authentic, that is my school.” And he said: “It is not permissible for anyone to take our word until he knows where we took it from.” Imam Malik, may Allah have mercy on him, said: “I am only a human being. I say what is right, and sometimes I also make mistakes. Look carefully at my opinions. Whatever opinion among them agrees with the Quran and the Sunnah, take it, and whatever opinion does not agree with the Quran and the Sunnah, leave it.”
