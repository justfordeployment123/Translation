# English source for book page 184

[en]
In this way this Ummah has been protected from gathering upon falsehood and misguidance. Nor is it possible for this Ummah to gather upon abandoning the truth.

[en]
Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah believe that no human being apart from the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, is protected from error. They hold that, in a complex matter, ijtihad may be exercised to the extent that is needed. Moreover, they are not partisan for any person's own opinion and ijtihad, nor are they his zealous supporters, unless his opinion is in accordance with the Quran and the Sunnah. They believe that the opinion of a mujtahid is sometimes right and that he also sometimes errs. If his opinion is right, he has two rewards, one reward for the ijtihad and one for giving the right opinion; but if he errs, he has only one reward, for the ijtihad.

[en]
According to Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, disagreement on matters of ijtihad neither gives rise to mutual enmity nor requires cutting off ties with one another. Rather, despite disagreeing on such matters, they love one another, keep friendship with one another and pray behind one another.

[en]
They do not make it binding on any Muslim to adhere to a particular school of jurisprudence (madhhab); however, they also see no harm in adhering to the school of a particular jurist, provided that this adherence is by way of following (ittiba') and not by way of taqlid (following an opinion without its evidence).

[fen]
Taqlid is that a legally accountable person (mukallaf), in a matter of a ruling of the Shariah, commits himself to the school of someone whose word is not in itself a proof.
