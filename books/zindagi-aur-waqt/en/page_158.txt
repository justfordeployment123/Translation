Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said: Remember often the destroyer of pleasures, that is, death.

[en]
And in Ibn Majah, Abdullah ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, narrated that he, peace be upon him, was asked:

[ar]
فَأَيُّ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أَكْيَسْ

[en]
Which of the believers is the wisest? He, peace be upon him, said:

[ar]
أَكْثَرُهُمْ لِلْمَوْتِ ذِكْرًا وَأَحْسَنُهُمْ لِمَا بَعْدَهُ اسْتِعْدَادًا أُولَئِكَ الأَكْيَاسْ

[ref]
سنن ابن ماجہ، رقم الحدیث: ۴۲۵۹

[en]
The one who remembers death the most, and prepares best for the life after death.

[en]
This narration shows that the one who remembers death is a wise and intelligent person, for in truth it is he who is making good use of the great blessing of life.

[hen]
An Example

[en]
To explain this matter further, consider this example. A man named Khalid has two friends, one named Umar and the other Hamid. Each of them contacted Khalid by phone and told him they were coming. Umar gave the date of his arrival: I will come in two months, on such and such a date. But Hamid gave no fixed date. Instead he said: I may come at any time, so be ready. Now, for whose arrival will Khalid prepare first? For whom will he arrange the room, the bed and other needs? Surely for Hamid, because he may come at any time. Now let a person apply this same example to death and the Day of Resurrection: the time of death has not been told, while the signs of the Hour and its coming have been made known. So a person should be prepared for death at all times.
