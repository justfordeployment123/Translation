[hen]
The Stranger

[en]
If you have ever lived away from home yourself, you will know this well. Otherwise, ask someone who lives abroad what life away from home is like. Consider the following.

[en]
Away from home, a person always keeps in mind that he will not stay there for good.

[en]
The days and nights of a person far from home pass in remembering his homeland.

[en]
A person far from home only ever arranges for himself the things without which he cannot get by.

[en]
Whatever wealth he gathers abroad, he gathers so that when he returns to his homeland he can live a good and comfortable life.

[en]
A person far from home never gives his heart for good to the people, places and houses of the foreign land.

[en]
In the same way, a believer should keep these points before him with regard to this world and the Hereafter, and see this world as a passing abode: this is a foreign land, and Paradise is my true homeland, to which I must return.

[hen]
One Passing Along a Road

[en]
Here the Prophet, peace be upon him, did not use the word traveller. Rather he said: one passing along a road, that is, a passer-by. For a traveller who stops somewhere for a few days during his journey is still a traveller. But the one who is walking on the road, moving steadily towards his destination, not stopping anywhere, advancing towards his goal every hour and every moment, is called one passing along a road. So instead of a traveller, the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, described the quality of one passing along a road, which carries greater emphasis and a stronger encouragement towards the Hereafter.
