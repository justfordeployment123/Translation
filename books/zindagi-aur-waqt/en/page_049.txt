Chapter Two: The Importance of Time in the Light of the Hadith

[hen]
The First Hadith

[ar]
عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ: مِنْ حُسْنِ إِسْلاَمِ الْمَرْءِ تَرْكُهُ مَا لاَ يَعْنِيهْ

[ref]
رواہ الترمذی، کتاب الزہد، رقم الحدیث: ۲۳۱۷، ابن ماجہ، کتاب الفتن، رقم الحدیث: ۳۹۷۶

[en]
Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said: Part of the excellence of a person's Islam is his leaving what does not concern him.

[en]
This saying of the Prophet, peace be upon him, is one of the basic principles of the religion, on which many of its matters depend, whether those matters concern individual life or society, married life or economics. Islam commands us to leave everything that is useless, pointless and without purpose. For a Muslim's life has a purpose, and a Muslim avoids whatever is purposeless.

[en]
For it is the Muslim who sees himself as a traveller moving towards his destination, spending every moment of his life on reaching it. The charming sights and fine resting places along the way do not make him forget his destination. Rather, guarding himself and turning away from the whisperings of Satan, from amusement and play, and from other useless things, he stays on his journey with great resolve and determination. Even the ups and downs of events and the highs and lows of life cannot break the firmness of his resolve, the speed of his pace, or its steadiness.
