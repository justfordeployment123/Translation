Imam Sufyan ath-Thawri, may Allah have mercy on him, said about his teacher Amr ibn Qays, may Allah have mercy on him: I learned manners, recitation and the laws of inheritance from him. Whenever I went looking for him, I would find him busy trading in the market. If he was not there, I would go to his house, where he would be found either praying voluntary prayers or reciting the Quran. And if he was not at home either, I would search the mosques of Kufah, and find him sitting in the corner of a mosque, weeping like a thief who hides and weeps in fear of being caught. And if he was not in the mosque either, he would be found sitting in a graveyard, saying to himself:

[en]
I do not put off today's work until tomorrow out of laziness, for surely the day of the helpless is always tomorrow.

[en]
Junayd al-Baghdadi, may Allah have mercy on him, advising a man, said: All goodness is gathered in three things.

[en]
One: if you cannot spend your day in something that is good for you, then do not spend it in something that will be a burden on you.

[en]
Two: if you do not keep the company of good people, then do not sit with bad people either.

[en]
Three: if you cannot spend your wealth in what pleases Allah, then do not spend it in what displeases Him either.
