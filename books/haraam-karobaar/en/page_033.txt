[en]
Ammar quickly gets up from his sleep, because he knows the truth that prayer is better than sleep. A person whose sleep grows long in this world will be overtaken by poverty on the Day of Resurrection. Ammar made wudu and set off for the mosque with great calm and dignity. He prayed Fajr in congregation in the first row, from the opening takbir, and then after the prayer he sat and recited the adhkar (words of remembrance).

[en]
Ammar had made an appointment with his teacher, Shaykh Salih, may Allah preserve him, so he went to him at the appointed time. But Shaykh Salih, after finishing the adhkar following the Fajr prayer, had prayed the Ishraq prayer and gone home, because he had forgotten the appointed time.

[en]
Ammar went to his teacher's house and knocked three times, but there was no answer. Ammar went back, because he knew that it is not permissible to ask for permission more than three times.

[en]
Then at eight o'clock in the morning Ammar went to the Shaykh again and knocked. The Shaykh came to the door laughing and cheerful, welcomed Ammar and took him into his office, then got up to bring breakfast.

[en]
Ammar: Shaykh! May Allah bless you and keep you in His protection and care. I have not come to eat or to ask for anything; rather, I have come to learn something from you.

[en]
Teacher: May Allah grant you right guidance. We have been commanded to honour our guests. Come, let us have breakfast. The two of them had breakfast together, and the Shaykh talked with his student in a very relaxed and friendly way.
