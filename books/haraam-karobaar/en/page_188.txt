Chapter Twenty-Three: On Games of Chance

[en]
The teacher was busy talking with the shopkeeper when Ammar saw a packet hanging in one of the shop's cabinets. It held small folded pieces of paper, and around it were quite a few items, banknotes and so on. Ammar turned to the shopkeeper.

[en]
Ammar: What is this packet with the little papers?

[en]
Shopkeeper: It is a game of chance.

[en]
Teacher: What does that mean?

[en]
Shopkeeper: Something is written on these papers, for example five rupees, a watch, or nothing at all. You can win from it, and you can also lose.

[en]
Teacher: Servant of Allah! May Allah bless you. This is a kind of gambling, and it is about this that Allah the Exalted says:

[ar]
إِنَّمَا الْخَمْرُ وَالْمَيْسِرُ وَالْأَنْصَابُ وَالْأَزْلَامُ رِجْسٌ مِنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونْ

[ref]
[المائدة: 90]

[en]
Indeed, intoxicants, gambling, (sacrificing on) stone alters (to other than Allah), and divining arrows are but defilement from the work of Satan, so avoid it that you may be successful.
