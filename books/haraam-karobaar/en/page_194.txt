Second: There is no element of good at all in throwing pebbles and the like; rather, it holds the makings of evil, mischief and fighting. For example, someone's tooth may be broken, an eye may be put out, and it may come to a fight.

[en]
Imam an-Nawawi says:

[en]
Al-khadhf means placing a pebble, a date stone or the like between two fingers and throwing it, and it involves many kinds of harm.

Third: No sane, adult and sensible Muslim can say that every kind of need makes every kind of means permissible.

[en]
Shopkeeper: Then what shall we say?

[en]
Teacher: We shall say that means and ways are of two kinds, and they have certain rulings:

[en]
Permissible means: These are the means whose ruling depends on the five rulings by which people are held accountable. The five rulings are: obligatory, forbidden, recommended, disliked and permissible. That is, if the need falls under the obligatory, then whatever serves as the means to it will also be obligatory; likewise, if the need is permissible, then taking up the means to it will also be permissible.
