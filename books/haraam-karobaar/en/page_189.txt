In the age of ignorance people used to gamble, and they had many forms of gambling. The best known of them was that ten men would buy a camel together. Then they would draw lots, and the seven of the ten whose names came out in the draw became equal owners of the camel, while the other three got nothing at all.

[en]
In our own time today there are also countless forms of gambling, for example:

[en]
First: Numbers are bought for money, and a token or a prize bond is given for them. Then, at a set time, a draw is held, one, two or three particular numbers are drawn from those issued, and prizes are given to them according to their rank.

[en]
Second: Goods are bought from a company. The company gives every buyer a token, and certain items are set aside as prizes. At a set time a draw is held, and whoever's name comes out gets the prize.

[en]
Third: Large clubs and sports centres have been set up. People go there, play games and gamble on them, such as video games, tennis and so on.

[en]
All the forms above are kinds of gambling and are forbidden, however fine a name we may give them.

[en]
Shopkeeper: So these games of chance and the like are a forbidden trade too?

[en]
Teacher: Yes, because when a thing is forbidden, trading in it is forbidden too.

[en]
Shopkeeper: I have heard this, and now I will act on it.
