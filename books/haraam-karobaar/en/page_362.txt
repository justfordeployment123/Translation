[en]
Teacher: What did you say? Will you really keep these two hundred pounds for yourself?

[en]
Muhammad: Yes. Did I not make the effort and take the trouble and toil of buying the freezer? Besides, Sufyan knows that a freezer of this quality and size costs two thousand five hundred pounds; that is why he sent that amount. I saved these two hundred pounds through my own cleverness and hard work, so I will keep them for myself, and they are lawful for me.

[en]
Teacher: Who told you that they are lawful for you?

[en]
Muhammad: What need is there to ask anyone else whether they are lawful or not? It is clear: the man has entrusted me with buying something, and he also knows its price. I have bought that very thing for him. It is no concern of his how much the thing cost; he just wants the item he asked for at that price, even if I buy it for him for two thousand.

[en]
Teacher: No, brother, this remaining money is not lawful for you in any way. The people of knowledge say that when a man appoints someone as his agent or representative, the agent must act in good faith for the one who appointed him.

[en]
Muhammad: Teacher! Do not be so strict. I told you, this is the fruit of my hard work. If my friend Sufyan had come and bought it himself, he would have got it for two thousand five hundred pounds anyway. And by the way, what proof do you have that it is forbidden?

[en]
Teacher: Come now, brother, remember one thing: the rulings of the Shariah should not be measured by reason and people's opinions.
