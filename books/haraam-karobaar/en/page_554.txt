Keeping this whole situation in view, the Council issues this fatwa: having oneself sterilised is forbidden, and preventing pregnancy for fear of the cost of providing for children or of other factors the Shariah does not recognise is forbidden, because in the Noble Quran Allah the Exalted says:

[ar]
هُوَ الرَّزَّاقُ ذُو الْقُوَّةِ الْمَتِينْ

[ref]
[الذاريات: 58]

[en]
Indeed, it is Allah who is the (continual) Provider, the firm possessor of strength.

[ar]
وَمَا مِنْ دَابَّةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا عَلَى اللَّهِ رِزْقُهَا

[ref]
[هود: 6]

And there is no creature on earth but that upon Allah is its provision.

[en]
Likewise, campaigning for this work, funding it, spending huge sums on it, and taking employment in this work and so on are also impermissible and forbidden. However, to cope with growing needs, it is necessary that the resources and sums being spent on the family planning campaign be spent instead on economic development and on improving people's poor economic condition.

[en]
Ammar: So in your view sterilisation and contraception are absolutely forbidden?

[en]
Teacher: No, they are not absolutely forbidden; rather, in some cases sterilisation and contraception are permissible. But this is to be applied case by case, looking at each woman's situation, to decide for which woman sterilisation is permissible and for which it is not. The final decision on this will be made by a skilled and honest doctor, keeping the woman's health in view.
