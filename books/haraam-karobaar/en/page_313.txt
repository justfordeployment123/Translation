Third: Al-Ahnaf ibn Qais was a very intelligent and wise man and the chief of his tribe. He had no beard on his face. The people of his tribe used to say: We wish we could buy a beard for our chief al-Ahnaf, even if we had to pay twenty thousand dirhams.

[en]
These incidents show that, among the early generations, having no beard was regarded as undesirable. These people were naturally without a beard, yet their longing and eagerness to have one was very great. They held that a beard brings beauty to a Muslim's person. In fact, there were some who thought having their necks cut easier than having their beards cut. But sadly, the importance of the beard for today's Muslim young man is such that he keeps spending thousands of rupees to destroy it. Do you know what the great scholar Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, said about it?

[en]
“Consider this: until the age of puberty, the appearance of a male and a female is alike, but after puberty the man stands apart from the woman because of his beard.

[en]
In fact, the nature of the responsibilities that Allah the Exalted has given to man requires that a man should have courage, boldness, strength, power, honour and dignity, and the beard is in harmony with all these requirements. On the other hand, the responsibilities that have been given to women require that a woman should have no beard on her face.”
