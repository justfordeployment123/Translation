Chapter Seventy-Two: The Prohibition of Making a Bequest for an Heir

[en]
Abdur-Rahim: Teacher! I do not agree with what you said, that there should be equality among children in gifts, donations and the like. In my view, if one child is given more wealth and another less, there is no harm in it. The second thing is that, as you know, my wife has passed away. Since then my only daughter has taken charge of all the affairs of the house, and she alone is my heir; apart from her I have no son or daughter. I also know that after my death my daughter's share of my inheritance is half, and the remaining half will go to my brothers, although I absolutely do not want even a single rupee of my inheritance to go to my brothers, because they have never treated me well. In the days when I was in trouble, they used to be delighted to see me in that state. So I do not want to deprive my dear daughter of the remaining half share and let it benefit people who are not entitled to even a single penny.
