Chapter Twenty-Six: Buying and Selling Explosives

[en]
The teacher and his student were about to leave the shop when a child came in.

[en]
Child: (speaking to the shopkeeper) Do you have bombs or firecrackers?

[en]
Teacher: (in surprise) Is this a general store or an arms shop?

[en]
Shopkeeper: (laughing) Teacher! This child is not asking for the kind of bomb you think. He is asking about the bomb that is a children's toy. (Then the shopkeeper turned to the child.) Yes, how many do you want?

[en]
Child: Give me ten bombs. (The shopkeeper quickly took them out and began to hand them over.)

[en]
Teacher: Brother! Wait a moment.

[en]
Shopkeeper: (laughing loudly) Here we go! Now one more thing is about to become forbidden!

[en]
Teacher: Yes, this too is forbidden, for several reasons.

[en]
Shopkeeper: What are those reasons?

[en]
Teacher: First: Its violent, loud noise harms people. Some are asleep, some are ill, some are travelling and driving with their minds on the road, and when they hear a firecracker or the like go off, they are suddenly frightened. When people light a firecracker and throw it, it may go off at someone's feet, and so on.
