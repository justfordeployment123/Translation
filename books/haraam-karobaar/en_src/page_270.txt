# English source for book page 270

[en]
I have asked him for it many times, and I have had our elders speak to him too, but he will not agree to give me my share. As the elder brother, he ought to have treated me with kindness, but he has shut the doors of his house to me and has gone as far as cutting off relations. When I come to his house, his family do not treat me well. I had thought, since my husband is a poor man, that I would take my share and give it to him so that he could do some business of his own and provide for us. (Khadijah broke down in tears as she spoke.)

[en]
Atiyyah: I am living in hardship myself. How can I give you a share?

[en]
(The teacher comforted Khadijah and said: Go home, rest and have something to eat; I will talk to Atiyyah.)

[en]
Teacher: Atiyyah! Do you not fear Allah as you wrong this poor, needy woman and devour her right? Are you unaware of where this injustice and wrongdoing will end?

[en]
How have you come to think this injustice lawful for yourself, dashing her hopes and causing her grief? Why do you not want your poor sister and her poor husband to benefit from your father's land? If your circumstances change and tomorrow someone treats you in the same way, will you put up with it?

[en]
Servant of Allah! Taking a share of the inheritance is your sister's right, and it has been made obligatory by Allah the Exalted. Just as you are your father's heir, she is an heir too. It is not as if you have to give her anything of your own!
