# English source for book page 370

[en]
In that age, the practice was that when the appointed time came, a man to whom another owed a loan would ask him: Do you want to pay off the loan, or do you want more time?

[en]
If you want more time, so much will be added to your loan. In the same way, when the appointed time came again, he would ask the same thing. Over three or four extensions the amount would grow considerably. Allah the Exalted has declared this the interest of the Age of Ignorance.

[en]
Faisal: But Teacher, what guarantee do I have that if an instalment is late I will not suffer a loss, and that I will get my due?

[en]
Teacher: There are two ways of doing that:

[en]
First: Take four instalments in advance from the customer, so that if a monthly instalment is late, you can count one of the advance instalments towards that month.

[en]
Second: Take some guarantee from the customer that he will not be late with an instalment.

[en]
Salah: There is also another form of instalment deal similar to this, in which the item is sold without the price being fixed. What is the ruling on that?

[en]
Teacher: What form is that?

[en]
Salah: That is, the customer says: Give me such-and-such a kind of freezer. The shopkeeper says: If you buy it in cash, it is one thousand pounds. Then the customer asks: How much is it in instalments? The shopkeeper answers: We do not fix a price for selling goods on instalments. Take the freezer, and on top of the original amount of one thousand, there will be a charge of thirty pounds a month. If you want to pay the money in two instalments, it is one thousand and sixty pounds, and if you want to pay it in four instalments, it is one thousand one hundred and twenty pounds. In the same way, however many instalments you want, there will be a charge of thirty pounds for each instalment.
