# English source for book page 74

[en]
I did not know anything about rice. I said to a shopkeeper: Give me number one hundred and two rice. He charged me the rate for that variety, but gave me rice of a lower variety, number four hundred. He deceived me because of my lack of knowledge.

[en]
Teacher: Do you know this man?

[en]
Fawzi: Yes, his shop is at the end of this market.

[en]
Teacher: Come! Let us give him some advice.

[en]
As soon as they reached him, Fawzi began to say loudly: You cheat! You deceived me. I asked for number one hundred and two rice, but you gave me number four hundred rice?

[en]
Shopkeeper: You have no grounds for complaint now. You should have looked before you bought.

[en]
Fawzi: I told you that I knew nothing about it. Shaykh, what do you think?

[en]
Shopkeeper: I have nothing to do with your Shaykh. You should have looked before buying.

[en]
Teacher: Is it really as Fawzi says?

[en]
Shopkeeper: He said very angrily: Yes!

[en]
Teacher: Is this permissible?

[en]
Shopkeeper: Yes, it is permissible. I showed them to him first.

[en]
Teacher: No; rather, this is forbidden. It is deception, and impermissible.

[en]
Shopkeeper: Do you have any proof for calling it forbidden?

[en]
Teacher: Yes, a great many proofs.
