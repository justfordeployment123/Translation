Teacher: Just as it was established from the earlier hadiths that in a case like the one above there must be two separate deals, another hadith also points to this.

[en]
Bilal, may Allah be pleased with him, came to the Prophet, peace be upon him, with dates of a fine kind. The noble Prophet, peace be upon him, asked: Where are these from? Bilal, may Allah be pleased with him, said: I had some poor dates; I sold two sa' of them and bought this one sa' of fine dates in exchange, so that I could feed the noble Prophet, peace be upon him. He, peace be upon him, said:

[en]
“Alas! Alas! This is interest itself. Do not do this. But when you intend to buy (fine dates), sell the poor ones and buy fine dates (with their price).”

[en]
Bilal, may Allah be pleased with him, had given a larger amount of poor dates and bought a smaller amount of fine dates in exchange; that is, there was inequality in things of one kind. In the same way, what you did was to take a hundred grams of old gold and give a hundred grams of new gold, but along with it you also took a price; in this case too there was inequality in things of one kind (gold). In the Shariah, this case falls into the category of the forbidden, and the noble Prophet, peace be upon him, described an excellent way of avoiding it: that is, first sell the poor dates, take their price, and then buy the fine dates. The two deals will then be separate. I hope you understand now.
