It is also a fact that, compared with the resources being put to use to reduce the population in Muslim countries and the attention being given to it in Islamic countries, the situation in Western countries is quite different. They do not publicise this campaign or spend money on it in their own countries the way they do in Muslim countries. And the irony is that the advertisements, posters and banners used in this campaign show scenes of children and parents that are completely made up and baseless, while other parents who see them take these pictures to be the truth. For example, on family planning posters, banners and the like, a picture is drawn of a woman whose clothes are dirty; her own state is also shown as dishevelled; from her face and appearance it seems as if she is a woman stricken with grief and troubles; and many children are seen around her in an extremely cramped house. The purpose of showing this whole scene is that viewers should think that this is the state a woman and a home are in because of having many children. But those people will never highlight this glimpse of Muslim society: a mother is sitting with great honour, dignity and splendour; one of her sons has come and fallen at her feet; a second son is kissing her head; a third son is feeding her; and a fourth son is kissing her hands.
