# English source for book page 356

[en]
Muntasir: As-salamu alaikum wa rahmatullah.

[en]
Teacher: Wa alaikum as-salam wa rahmatullah.

[en]
Muntasir: I know why you people have called me.

[en]
Faisal: Tell us, then, why have we called you?

[en]
Muntasir: To talk about the same subject you are always talking about.

[en]
Salah: Which subject?

[en]
Muntasir: The subject of putting in parts.

[en]
Faisal: Yes. Up to now, has anything we have said convinced you that this is forbidden work?

[en]
Muntasir: How can I believe that it is forbidden work? The customer just needs to get his item back repaired; he does not care what has been put into it and what has been taken out.

[en]
Teacher: Come now, brother! The aim is not just to repair the item; it is also necessary to tell him which parts you have put in it: genuine, imitation, fake, durable, not durable, new or old. Otherwise it will be adulteration and deception, that you charge the price of a new part and put in an old one.

[en]
Muntasir: Teacher! This is a means of earning a living, and in my view the important thing is that the item should be working, and that is what the customer wants.

[en]
Teacher: Muntasir! This justification of yours goes against the Shariah, for the following reasons:

[en]
First: In business and in buying and selling, the nature, kind, price and so on of the goods must be completely clear, and both parties must agree on them.
