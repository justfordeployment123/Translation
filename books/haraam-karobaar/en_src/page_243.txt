# English source for book page 243

[en]
Abdul-Wahhab: Teacher! From what you say, it seems that giving tuition is a crime.

[en]
Ubaid: (laughing) Abdul-Wahhab has become worried because his son is also a schoolteacher.

[en]
Teacher: I do not consider giving tuition a crime, but this much is certain: this practice is permissible on a few conditions:

[en]
First: The teacher should do such justice to the lesson at school that the children no longer need to take tuition.

[en]
Second: The teacher should not force the children into tuition, either openly or by hints; however, if the children themselves show that they are willing to take tuition, there is no harm.

[en]
Third: The teacher should give equal attention to all the children in class and respect the rights of every child.

[en]
Fourth: He should keep in mind the needs and problems of poor students.

[en]
That is, if a child is poor and cannot meet the costs of education, the teacher should help lighten his burden, because Allah the Exalted says:

[en]
And if someone is in hardship, then (let there be) postponement until (a time of) ease.

[en]
Fifth: The teacher should not make a distinction between rich and poor in class, but should treat everyone alike.

[en]
In the matter of tuition, when all these conditions are met, then it is lawful, and its earnings, fees and so on are lawful, and there is nothing wrong in it. However, if even one of the above conditions is missing, it must be avoided; otherwise it will come under the heading of the forbidden.
