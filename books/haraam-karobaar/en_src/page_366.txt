# English source for book page 366

[en]
Teacher: But do check whether making a deal in this way is even permissible.

[en]
Muhammad: Teacher! Is this form forbidden too, like the earlier one?

[en]
Teacher: First of all, we need to know what this form of dealing is called in the terminology of the Shariah.

[en]
Faisal: Teacher! You tell us what it is called.

[en]
Teacher: Among the scholars this form is called bay'atayn fi bay'ah (making two deals in one deal); that is, the seller says to the buyer: This item is one thousand in cash and two thousand on credit; you have the choice of buying it in cash or on credit. Then the buyer takes the item away, but the two of them do not settle whether the deal has been made in cash or on credit.

[en]
Buying and selling goods in this way is invalid and impermissible, because this way the deal remains vague and undecided, whereas in buying and selling it is necessary that the deal be clear and settled, with no kind of vagueness or anything left undecided in it. This is just how the two of you have made your deal now: Muhammad takes the AC away and will decide at home whether to take it in cash or in instalments; if the money can be arranged, then in cash, and otherwise in instalments.

[en]
Muhammad: Teacher! What is the position of the scholars on this?

[en]
Teacher: The scholars say that this form is forbidden for the following two reasons:

[en]
First: In this form the price is not fixed but is left hanging; that is, in the very sitting in which the deal is being made, it is not settled whether the deal is in cash or in instalments.
