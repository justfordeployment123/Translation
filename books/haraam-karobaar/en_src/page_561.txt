# English source for book page 561

[en]
Fifth prize: A suitable gift.

[en]
Teacher! Is it permissible to hold competitions in this way?

[en]
Teacher: No, holding competitions in this way is completely impermissible.

[en]
Ammar: Why?

[en]
Teacher: Because the prize money in it is not clear.

[en]
In our view, the correct conditions for holding a competition are as follows:

[en]
First: The prize money should be completely clear; it is not right to announce vague kinds of prizes.

[en]
If the prizes are in the form of money, it should be stated at the start that the one who wins first prize will be given such-and-such an amount, the second such-and-such, and so on.

[en]
If the prizes are in the form of books, it should be stated beforehand that the one who comes first will be given such-and-such books, and likewise the second, the third and so on, so that those taking part in the competition can think it over and be satisfied before joining it, and work hard accordingly. It should not be that those taking part work very hard and take great pains, and then, when the turn of the prizes comes, they are disappointed and it does not even cover what they spent out of their own pockets.

[en]
Second: The things chosen as prizes should be permissible and lawful.

[en]
Things that are forbidden in themselves, such as a guitar and the like, should not be included.

[en]
Ammar: Teacher! Where we live, one common form of competition is this: a competition is announced in the mosque, and whoever wants to take part pays ten pounds. With whatever amount is collected in this way, ten prizes are later bought, and these are shared out among those who take the first ten places.
