# English source for book page 241

[en]
As for tuition, to this day I have never forced tuition on any student. I too have a conscience, and the fear and dread of Allah within me.

[en]
Tal'at: Servant of Allah! If you do not force them, then why do dozens of students stand in front of your house every evening carrying their books?

[en]
Isam: They come of their own free will. Teacher! Is there anything wrong in that?

[en]
Teacher: Isam! It is very good that you do not tell any student to take tuition by force, but, as Brother Tal'at has said, hundreds of other teachers use force in this matter. I want to put this request to them: not paying proper attention to the children's lessons in class merely for one's own interest, and forcing children to come to oneself for tuition outside class, when the children are not happy to take tuition and the parents are not happy to have them taught, or the parents do not have enough income to pay private fees for their children's teaching, is impermissible and forbidden.

[en]
Have you ever considered how many poor people's children are deprived of the blessing of knowledge because of a teacher's injustice? Poor parents are not able to bear the cost of tuition, and because of that they cannot get their children educated. A most truthful and honest friend of mine told me the story of just such a cruel and greedy teacher. He used to force the students of his school to come to him for tuition in the second session. Among those students was a poor boy. His father had died, and his mother was feeding herself and her children by doing menial work. She longed for her child to get a higher education, but that old woman did not have the means to meet the tuition fees and other expenses of that cruel teacher. In the end, that hard-pressed, helpless, poverty-stricken mother, worn out by that greedy teacher, took her child out of school, and so his education was cut off.
